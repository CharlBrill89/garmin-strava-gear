The drags

Both climbs
These are not hills Both climbs start at a turnaround: climb 1 runs about 5 km from just before km 5, climb 2 about 5.5 km from km 15.5. They average 0.7–0.9%, but that average hides the shape. In 250 m pieces they come as 1.5–2.6% pitches with flatter bits and short dips between, a 2.5–3.7% start just after each turn, and a 3–5% kick near each top. Run them at even effort: let the pace slow on the pitches and take it back on the flat bits, so the climb as a whole averages Plan A. Don't fight to hold 5:17 up a kick. Losing 12–15 s/km on a drag this long is correct, not a failure.
The spike you feel is not the one that counts On a 2.5–3% pitch your HR jumps 10–17 bpm within a few hundred metres, and both climbs have a few of those, so expect spikes on the pitches and the kick that settle on the flatter bits. Over a whole climb the drift is smaller. Across 111 of your kilometre laps the relationship is only +1.8 bpm per 1% of gradient, because you already slow by about 5 s/km per 1% without thinking about it. Brushing 170 as you crest something is nothing; sitting at 170 for three minutes is the problem. Judge it on the rolling average.
Climb 1 is the rehearsal Km 5–10 tells you exactly how km 15.5–21 will feel, five kilometres before it counts. Read it honestly at the 10 km gate.
Km 14 is the one free kilometre A 26 m drop in a single kilometre. Let it run — it should be your fastest split of the day and it costs almost nothing.
Short stride on both drops Km 3 and km 14 are where your quads get the bill. Reaching out downhill multiplies the braking force they absorb, and that is what is burning at the end of your runs. Quick turnover, short stride, stay relaxed — let gravity do it rather than catching yourself. You want those legs intact for the climb from km 15.5.
Km 18 is the crisis The steepest kilometre of the race, +17 m, with 18 km in your legs. Your slowest 2024 split landed exactly here. Expect it to hurt, know it is one kilometre, and know that in 2024 you still came home at 5:11.

If HR looks wrong

Recovered, but keep the rule
The gate showed HR back to normal: 146 on a stretch you ran at 151 before the illness. A month after a three-week illness it can still misreport on a bad day, commonly 5–10 bpm high at a given pace, occasionally blunted and low while you feel awful.
  • High HR at easy effort → trust the HR, back off.
  • Low HR but laboured breathing → trust the breathing, back off.
Asymmetric on purpose: whichever signal says slow down wins.
